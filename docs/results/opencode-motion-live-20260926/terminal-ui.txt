Punch Trainer: Target Dummy Drill

Saved proposal · revision 1

Mechanics

Core loop: the player character stands within punching range of a stationary target dummy. Pressing a designated input (default: left mouse click / gamepad X / spacebar) triggers a punch action if the player is not already mid-punch (no input buffering, single-action lockout during animation). On successful contact with the dummy's hitbox, three things fire in sync: (1) the punch animation completes its strike frame, (2) a punch/hit sound effect plays once, (3) an on-screen hit counter increments by 1. The counter persists for the session and displays in a fixed HUD position (top-center by default). A simple cooldown equal to the punch animation duration prevents multiple counter increments from a single button press (one press = one animation cycle = at most one hit registered). The dummy has a static collider and does not react physically (no knockback, no health depletion) beyond a lightweight visual cue (e.g., a brief flash or wobble) to give the player hit feedback beyond sound/counter alone. Player movement is optional and minimal: player may be locked in place facing the dummy, or allowed limited forward/back movement to approach; combat itself is a single-button timing/spacing drill, not a multi-move combo system. No damage numbers, no dummy defeat state, no win/lose condition — this is a practice/arcade counter loop, not a full fight scene.

Assumptions: Single player only, no AI opponent or multiplayer. Dummy is fully stationary and cannot be destroyed, defeated, or moved. One punch animation/type is sufficient; no combo strings, blocking, or special moves required. Hit detection is a simple overlap/trigger check between the player's punch hitbox and the dummy's static collider, resolved once per animation cycle. Counter has no upper limit and does not reset automatically; a manual reset (e.g., on scene reload) is acceptable default behavior. No scoring system, timer, or high-score persistence beyond the current session is required. Camera is fixed or simple follow-cam; no complex camera work needed. Input lockout during the punch animation is desired to prevent counter exploitation via rapid spam clicking.

Unresolved: Should the hit counter reset on scene reload/restart, or persist across sessions (e.g., saved high score)? Is any player movement required (walk toward dummy) or is the player fixed in a single spot facing the dummy at all times?

Theme

Lighthearted training-gym aesthetic centered on a single practice activity: punching a heavy bag styled target dummy. Tone is casual and skill-focused rather than violent or narrative-driven — closer to a rhythm/timing drill than a combat story. The player character can be a generic boxer/fighter archetype (simple silhouette or low-poly humanoid is sufficient); no specific character identity, backstory, or dialogue is needed. The target dummy is themed as a classic canvas-and-leather punching dummy (freestanding base, no face required, though a simple painted face or stitched pattern adds charm). Visual and audio feedback should feel satisfying and arcade-like: a crisp 'thwack' sound, a quick screen-shake or dummy wobble, and a counter that pops or ticks up energetically to reward consistent hitting. No blood, no injury depiction, no aggressive tone — this is a fitness/training vibe, suitable for all ages.

Assumptions: Setting is a generic gym/dojo/training room rather than a specific licensed or narrative universe. No spoken dialogue, story cutscenes, or character customization are in scope. Visual style defaults to simple/stylized (flat-shaded or low-poly) rather than photorealistic, keeping asset scope light. Punch/hit sound effect is a single stock-style 'thwack' or 'thump'; no voice grunts or combo-specific audio required. Dummy design is a single reusable visual (no multiple dummy skins or difficulty variants).

Environment & layout

A small, single-room training space just large enough to comfortably frame the player and the dummy in view. Recommended layout: a flat ground plane (indoor gym floor or outdoor dirt-yard, either is fine) with the target dummy fixed at a set point roughly one arm's-reach-plus-buffer from the player's default standing position, ensuring the punch animation's reach lines up with the dummy's hitbox without requiring precise positioning by the player. Camera defaults to a fixed side-on or three-quarter angle that keeps both the player and dummy fully in frame at all times — no camera panning or player-controlled camera needed given the confined, single-purpose space. HUD placement: hit counter anchored top-center or top-right of the screen in a clear, readable font, always visible during gameplay. Lighting is simple and even (single directional/ambient light setup) since this is a small enclosed or semi-enclosed practice area, not requiring dynamic time-of-day or weather. No additional props, NPCs, or background activity are necessary; the space should stay uncluttered so player focus remains entirely on the punch-and-count loop.

Assumptions: Environment is a single static scene/level with no level progression, additional rooms, or scene transitions. Fixed camera angle is acceptable; no free-look or player-controlled camera rotation is required. No background NPCs, decorative crowd, or ambient scene activity beyond the player and dummy are needed. Lighting can be simple/static; no day-night cycle or dynamic weather system is in scope. Dummy's fixed position is chosen once during setup and does not need runtime repositioning or multiple placement options.
