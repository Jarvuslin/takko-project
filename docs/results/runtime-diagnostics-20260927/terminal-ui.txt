Punch the Dummy: Combat Trainer

Proposal approved · revision 1

Mechanics

The player controls a standard Roblox character standing near a single stationary target dummy. Pressing an attack input (default: left mouse click or a designated key) triggers a punch: the player's punch animation plays, and a hit check determines whether the punch connects with the dummy's hitbox (via touch detection or a short-range proximity/raycast check in front of the player). If the punch connects, the hit sound plays immediately, and an on-screen numeric counter increments by one. The counter is displayed persistently in a corner of the screen throughout play. The dummy is fully stationary and non-aggressive: it does not move, retaliate, take damage state changes, or reset, and there is no fail state, health system, or win condition beyond accumulating hits. The loop is a simple, repeatable practice cycle: approach, punch, hear/see feedback, watch the counter climb. A short cooldown between successive punches (e.g., matching the punch animation length) prevents input spam from firing multiple hits per single swing.

Assumptions: The player uses the default Roblox R15 humanoid character with no custom rig requirements. The attack input is a simple, single keybind or mouse click with no combo or charge system. Hit detection uses a short-range touch or proximity check between the player's punching hand/arm and the dummy's hitbox. The on-screen counter is a basic UI label with no persistence across sessions and no leaderboard integration. There is exactly one target dummy in the world; no spawning, respawning, or multiple targets are required. A brief per-punch cooldown equal to the animation duration prevents duplicate hit registration from a single swing.

Theme

A minimal, arcade-style fighting/training theme centered on a boxing or martial-arts practice space. The tone is casual and skill-focused rather than narrative-driven: the player is a fighter honing technique against a static training dummy, similar to a gym heavy-bag station. Visual and audio style leans toward gritty, energetic combat-sport aesthetics (gym mats, tape-wrapped dummy, punchy impact sound) rather than fantasy or sci-fi framing, keeping the experience approachable and immediately understandable.

Assumptions: The setting is a realistic-leaning gym/dojo training theme rather than a fantasy or sci-fi combat theme. No opponent AI, story, or progression narrative is implied beyond simple practice against a dummy. Color palette and materials should read as a training facility (mats, tape, matte dummy) rather than an ornate arena.

Environment & layout

A single small enclosed room or platform serving as a practice arena, roughly 20x20 studs, with a flat floor (mat-textured if available) and simple walls or boundary markers to keep the player near the dummy. The player spawns near the center of the room, facing the target dummy which is anchored a few studs away at approximately (0, 3, 15) so it stands upright on the floor. The on-screen hit counter UI is anchored to a fixed corner of the screen (e.g., top-right) and remains visible at all times during play. Lighting is simple and even, sufficient to clearly see the dummy and read punch feedback; no complex weather, day/night cycle, or multi-room layout is needed. The space is intentionally compact and free of obstacles so the punch-and-feedback loop remains uninterrupted.

Assumptions: A single-room practice arena is sufficient; no multi-level or outdoor environment is required. The dummy's fixed position near the room center is adequate for consistent hit-range access. Standard baseplate-style lighting and materials are acceptable; no custom skybox or weather system is needed. The UI counter sits in a fixed screen corner and does not need to be repositioned dynamically.
