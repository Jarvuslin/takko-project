Punch the Dummy: Target Practice

Proposal approved · revision 2

Mechanics

Core loop: the player character stands within melee range of a single stationary target dummy and performs a punch action on player input (default binding: left mouse button or a designated 'Attack' button, e.g. E on keyboard / West face button on gamepad). Triggering the input plays a single punching animation on the player rig. Hit detection is resolved once per punch swing: a short-range forward hitbox/raycast is checked against the dummy's collider during the active frames of the punch animation. If the check registers contact with the dummy, exactly one 'hit' event fires: (1) a punch/impact sound effect plays, and (2) an on-screen hit counter increments by one. If the punch animation plays but does not connect with the dummy (player out of range or facing away), no sound plays and the counter does not increment. The dummy is immobile, has no health value, cannot be destroyed, and does not react beyond being the passive target of hit checks — it never resets, depletes, or triggers any secondary state. The counter is a simple persistent on-screen numeric display (e.g. 'Hits: 0') that only ever increases across the session; it is not a score, timer, combo multiplier, or damage value, and has no upper limit or reset condition. Input is rate-limited only by the natural duration of the punch animation (i.e., the player cannot start a new punch until the current animation finishes), preventing overlapping hit registrations from a single button press but adding no cooldown, combo, or stamina system beyond this animation-locking behavior.

Assumptions: Single player character controllable via standard movement (walk/run) plus one dedicated punch input; no additional attack types are added. Punch input default: left mouse click (PC) or a single mapped gamepad button; rebinding is out of scope unless requested later. One dummy exists in the scene at a fixed position and orientation; it does not move, rotate, or respawn. Hit detection uses a simple forward hitbox or short raycast from the player's fist/hand bone active only during the punch animation's strike frames. Exactly one hit-and-counter increment can occur per completed punch animation cycle, even if the hitbox theoretically overlaps the dummy for multiple frames. The punch/hit sound is a single audio clip with no variation, layering, or randomization requested. The on-screen counter is a plain numeric text UI element with no styling, animation, or visual feedback beyond updating the number. No cooldown, stamina, or resource system limits punching beyond the animation's own playback duration.

Theme

Minimal training/practice setting with no narrative framing. The player character is a generic humanoid fighter (default: a neutral, unbranded boxer or martial-artist model) whose sole visual expression of theme is the punching animation itself. The target dummy is depicted as a classic practice dummy (e.g., a padded post-and-base training dummy or heavy bag silhouette) to visually communicate its role as a passive striking target. No storyline, characters, dialogue, победа/loss conditions, or thematic progression are included, consistent with the request to exclude combos, health, enemies, and rounds.

Assumptions: Player model and dummy model use simple, generic 'training gym' visual styling by default (no licensed characters, no narrative skins). No voiceover, dialogue, or story text is included anywhere in the experience. Theme is limited to reinforcing the single mechanical loop (punch → hit → sound → counter) with no additional thematic systems (no ranks, titles, or unlockables).

Environment & layout

A single, static, enclosed practice space (default: a small indoor gym room or open flat platform) sized just large enough to contain the player's starting position and the dummy's fixed position within comfortable punching range, with no additional rooms, props, or navigable areas. Layout: player spawns facing the dummy at a fixed short distance (default ~1–1.5 meters) so the punch range check reliably connects when the player is stationary and facing forward; the dummy is anchored at a single fixed point in front of the spawn and never relocates. Camera defaults to a standard third-person follow camera centered on the player, oriented toward the dummy at spawn, with no cutscenes, camera switching, or cinematic framing. Lighting is simple and uniform (default flat/ambient lighting) sufficient to clearly see the punch animation and dummy; no dynamic lighting effects, particle environments, or weather are included. There is exactly one dummy in the environment and no other interactive or decorative elements beyond basic floor/wall geometry needed to bound the space.

Assumptions: Environment is a single small static scene with no level transitions, checkpoints, or multiple areas. Player spawn point and dummy position are both fixed and pre-placed; no randomization of layout. Standard third-person (or first-person, if simpler for punch-animation visibility) camera is acceptable as default; exact camera perspective is a low-impact implementation detail. Basic bounding geometry (floor and optional walls) is included only to give the space a sense of place, not as a gameplay mechanic (no walls-as-obstacles, no boundaries that affect scoring).

EXPERIENCE DIRECTION
A minimal single-player fighting-practice loop. From spawn, the player stands in a small enclosed gym room a short distance from a fixed practice dummy, viewed from the default third-person camera. Pressing the punch input (left mouse / E / gamepad West) plays a punching animation on the player's rig and locks further punch input until that animation finishes. During the animation's strike window the server checks a short forward hit region against the dummy's fixed collider. If it connects, the server increments that player's persistent hit count and tells their client to play a punch/impact sound and update an on-screen 'Hits: N' counter; if it does not connect, nothing plays and the counter does not change. The dummy never reacts, moves, resets, or depletes. Continuing to land punches is the only goal: the counter only ever climbs, giving the player a simple, immediate reason to keep swinging. No VFX, combos, health, enemies, rounds, menus, multiplayer scoring, or damage numbers are added — exactly the four requested elements (animation, hit-only sound, counter, stationary dummy) are implemented. Assumptions per the approved proposal: single dedicated punch input (no rebinding), one fixed dummy, one static gym-style room, default third-person camera and flat ambient lighting, and per-player (not shared/competitive) hit counts since multiplayer scoring is explicitly excluded but multiple players may still be present in the server.

Small, self-contained gym/practice room: flat matte grey/beige concrete floor and plain walls, uniform ambient lighting with no dynamic effects, no clutter or secondary props. The practice dummy is the sole focal object, centered a short distance in front of the fixed spawn/facing direction so a stationary, forward-facing player reliably lands the hit check. Player rig is a generic, unbranded default humanoid; its only thematic expression is the punching animation itself. Camera is the standard Roblox third-person follow camera, framed toward the dummy at spawn with no cinematic switching. The on-screen counter is a plain, unstyled numeric text element ('Hits: 0') in a fixed HUD position, updating only its number with no animation, color change, or decoration. No particle, glow, or trail effects anywhere.
