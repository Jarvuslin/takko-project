## What the context test shows

The decisions preserve meaningful genre-specific context: dummy health/flinch and combo contact; individual bubble popping, post-completion counting and reset; ordered checkpoint progression and fall recovery. Queries are short enough to find relevant components. That is evidence of context reaching discovery across three genres. It does not prove full game understanding, good asset selection after source review, successful integration or native player experience.

The plans still emphasize geometry reuse and anticipate custom behavior too early:

- Combat's strategy says to search animations, but its executable asset needs contain a dummy and audio only; it already proposes procedural keyframing. No complete combat/animation component search occurs before the first capability stop.
- Bubble-wrap's need lists base mesh, domes and materials; the worker recognizes an interactive ASMR component, but discusses adding Tween/state logic before inspecting whether it already exists.
- Parkour lists checkpoint and moving-platform geometry; the selection reason anticipates adding server/Tween behavior. The run stops at the first checkpoint, so the moving-platform need is unexecuted.

These are planning/coverage weaknesses, separate from the product importer failure. Simply repeating “Marketplace first” or increasing the dollar cap has not solved them. A follow-up must defer replacement decisions until the component's reviewed behavior and integration interfaces are known, then measure which requested features were actually reused.
